## The TMDB fill feature

The bots now propose the work that fills a TMDB gap: when TMDB lists more episodes in a season than all our works map, and exactly one other work of the same TVDB or TMDB show has no TMDB mapping, is finished and has that many episodes, a tier B PR maps it after our last mapped episode (`data/seeds/tmdb-splits/<work>.json`, optional `tmdb_id`). It is only ever a hint; a person confirms the episode on TMDB. Tests: `packages/bots/test/tmdb-fill.test.ts`. Issue #90 was the pattern; it was fixed by hand before the weekly run.

## The 2026-10-25 proxy export

Xwolv's export (data 2026.43.6) had 500 names (the export cap) from 10,722 sightings and no assisted names. Replayed against current main: **1 name resolved before the fixes, 57 after (2,625 sightings)**. The other 443 names are ambiguous or unmatched for the reasons below.

Parser and core fixes, each with tests:

- HTML entities are decoded (`Ranma1.&amp;.2`).
- Amazon's `Original Language` marker before the episode is dropped (Saint Tail).
- `hybrid` and DVD scene tags (FRA, PAL, DVD5, BD25/50) are metadata, so a movie's tags after its year leave the title (Pompo the Cinephile).
- `Title.[2026]-03` is episode 3 (Magic Knight Rayearth, Keroro Gunsou).
- A trailing year plus special marker in a no-episode release leaves the title (`Iczer One 1985 OVA`).
- `&` normalizes to `and` (Welsh & Shedar).
- Aliases: `Our Rain Color Protocol`, `Ranma1&2` (the season token then picks the season on the 2024 work).
- `data/groups/erai-raws.yaml`: Erai-raws numbers Steel Ball Run by the TV season, so `Steel Ball Run-06` is season 6 episode 6, the 2nd stage's episode 5 (offset 1). A group override now also settles the "no season token reads as the first season" rule, because the override states how that group numbers the work (`viaGroupOverride` in `packages/core/src/resolve.ts`).

Corpus: the export's 500 names are in `tests/releases.yaml` (`source: proxy-unresolved`). 57 are checked and verified against the release's own SxxExx, 443 keep the resolver's answer of the day and are unverified. Because the corpus fails when a case expected ambiguous or unmatched comes back ok, the unverified cases are a watch list: when data or parser work makes one resolve, CI names it. That is the intended loop; update the case and verify it.

The npm workflow also got a fix: its dry run failed on a version already on npm (npm 11 checks the registry even with `--dry-run`), so any PR touching `packages/core|parser|client` was red. The dry-run branch now skips published versions.
